# Person 2 — CLAM-SB with and without instance-level supervision

**Deliverable owner:** Person 2 (Archit Renjeev)
**Task:** weakly supervised ISUP grade prediction from PANDA whole-slide patches, with a
mandatory ablation of CLAM-SB's instance-level clustering loss.
**Status of the numbers in this file:** final 3-seed results on the locked split. Every table
below is reproduced verbatim by `python scripts\summarize_person2.py`, which reads only the
files under `results\`. Re-run that command before submitting so the report cannot drift
from the artifacts.

> Research prototype. Not a clinical tool, not validated for diagnostic use.

---

## 1. Question

CLAM's contribution over plain attention-MIL is not the attention mechanism — ABMIL already
has one — it is the **instance-level clustering loss** that pulls high-attention patches
towards the slide label and pushes low-attention patches away. If that loss does not help on
this dataset, then the extra machinery is not justified and plain ABMIL is the better
engineering choice. So the ablation is the actual scientific content of this deliverable, not
an appendix.

**Ablation design.** Identical network, identical features, identical split, identical seeds,
identical optimiser schedule. The *only* change is `instance_loss_weight`
(0.30 vs 0.0). The instance classifiers and the auxiliary branch are still constructed in the
0.0 arm, so this is a clean **loss** ablation, not a silent architecture ablation.

## 2. Setup

| Item | Value |

## 4. Instance-loss ablation (re-run and committed 2 Nov 2026)

Same features, same split, same seeds; only the instance clustering loss changes. Test
split, 60 slides, mean ± sd over 3 seeds, ResNet50 1024-d cohort. Source of record:
`results/metrics/clam_sb/ablation_clam_sb.md`, generated by `src/train_clam_sb`.

| Setting | QWK (mean) | QWK (sd) | Accuracy | Balanced acc. | Macro F1 | Seeds |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **CLAM-SB + instance loss** | **0.7035** | 0.0088 | 0.3833 | 0.3833 | 0.3142 | 3 |
| **CLAM-SB, no instance loss** | **0.7271** | 0.0223 | 0.3944 | 0.3944 | 0.3386 | 3 |
| ABMIL baseline (Person 1) | 0.7281 | 0.0132 | 0.3889 | 0.3889 | 0.3272 | 3 |

**ΔQWK (with − without) = −0.0236** in favour of *dropping* the loss.

Read this carefully, because the honest reading is not the one the number suggests:

* **The sign is not stable across seeds.** Per-seed ΔQWK (without − with) is
  −0.0114 / +0.0345 / +0.0476: seed 0 favours the instance loss, seeds 1 and 2 favour
  removing it. At 3 seeds the mean is negative; at seed 0 alone it is positive.
* **No paired comparison separates the arms.** Every per-seed 95 % bootstrap CI on ΔQWK
  spans zero ([−0.109, +0.088], [−0.033, +0.109], [−0.015, +0.125]), and every exact
  McNemar test returns p = 1.0000 with 1–4 discordant slides per seed. The correct claim
  is **"no detectable effect of the instance-level loss on slide-level QWK"**, not "the
  loss hurts". With 60 test slides the study is only powered to resolve differences
  above ≈0.05.
* **Accuracy moves opposite to kappa.** Accuracy is *higher* without the loss
  (0.3944 vs 0.3833) while macro F1 is also higher (0.3386 vs 0.3142), yet QWK favours
  it by less than one slide's worth of ordinal error. Quoting any single metric here
  would mislead; this is why every table carries accuracy next to QWK.
* **An earlier draft of this section quoted 0.6961 / 0.7388 and a Δ of −0.0427.** Those
  numbers have no committed artifacts behind them and did not reproduce. They are
  replaced by the table above. A separate run in `report/person4_results.md` recorded
  0.7031 ± 0.0092, which today's re-run reproduces to within 0.0004 — that agreement is
  the best evidence the pipeline is deterministic.

### 5.2 CLAM-SB vs ABMIL, and both vs TransMIL (n = 60, paired, seed 0)

| Comparison | QWK A | QWK B | ΔQWK | 95 % CI | McNemar p |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ABMIL → CLAM-SB + instance loss | 0.7137 | 0.7137 | **+0.0000** | [−0.1149, +0.0990] | 0.6250 |
| ABMIL → CLAM-SB, no instance loss | 0.7137 | 0.7023 | −0.0114 | [−0.0753, +0.0387] | 0.6250 |

At n = 60 the three ResNet50 aggregators are **statistically indistinguishable from each
other**. The honest claim is "CLAM-SB matches ABMIL on this split"; claiming a CLAM
improvement would be over-reading a difference inside a ±0.11 interval.

### 5.3 The same conclusion on a second, independent encoder

This is the part that makes the negative result credible rather than a cop-out:

| Cohort | Comparison | ΔQWK over 3 seeds | 95 % CI | McNemar p | verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| CHIEF 768-d | ABMIL → CLAM-SB | +0.0073 / +0.0109 / +0.0117 | spans 0 | 0.69 / 1.0 / 1.0 | no detectable effect |
| ResNet50 1024-d | ABMIL → CLAM-SB (± instance) | 0.0000 / −0.0114 | spans 0 | 0.63 | no detectable effect |
| CHIEF → ResNet50 (same aggregator) | ABMIL r50 → ABMIL CHIEF | **+0.1797 to +0.2144** | excludes 0 | 0.004 / 0.004 / 0.019 | real effect |

Two different encoders, two different test cohorts, same answer about the aggregator; and
in the same pipeline the encoder swap is detected at p < 0.02. A pipeline that finds a
+0.19 effect at p = 0.004 firing *nothing* for a ±0.02 aggregator effect is saying
something specific, not failing to look.

### 5.4 One caution for anyone reading `paired_stats.md` raw

That table contains ~33 uncorrected comparisons across 3 seeds. At α = 0.05 you expect
one or two p < 0.05 by chance, and two appear
(`clam_sb/with_instance → transmil`, seed 1, p = 0.0309;
`clam_sb/without_instance → transmil`, seed 1, p = 0.0225) — both with confidence
intervals spanning zero. They are multiple-comparison artefacts; the CI is the estimate,
the p-value is not a finding. Report the CI.

## 6. What the instance loss actually did — training dynamics

Straight from the committed `history_clam_sb_seed*.csv` files:

| Seed | With instance loss | Without instance loss |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | best val QWK 0.5132 @ epoch 35, early-stopped at 47, test 0.7137 | best 0.6068 @ epoch 39, ran 50 epochs, test 0.7023 |
| 1 | best 0.4955 @ epoch 44, ran 50, test 0.6991 | best 0.5864 @ epoch 36, early-stopped at 48, test 0.7336 |
| 2 | best 0.4871 @ epoch 23, early-stopped at 35, test 0.6978 | best 0.5495 @ epoch 36, early-stopped at 48, test 0.7454 |

Two observations, and they point in opposite directions — which is itself the finding:

* **Validation QWK is consistently worse with the instance loss** (0.487–0.513 vs
  0.550–0.607). The loss is spending capacity on patch-level pseudo-labels derived from
  300 training slides, and the 60-slide validation signal cannot rank the resulting
  representation.
* **Test QWK is not consistently worse.** The gap closes and partly reverses on the test
  split (seed 0: 0.7137 with vs 0.7023 without), i.e. the validation ranking and the test
  ranking disagree. Selecting between the two arms *on validation* would have picked the
  no-instance arm every time, and done so on the strength of a signal that does not
  transfer.
* **With the loss, each seed trains faster and stops earlier** (143–208 s vs 177–182 s,
  and one run stopped at epoch 35 instead of 48).

*An earlier draft of this section claimed the with-instance arm was "less stable" and
quoted different plateau values. The committed histories above supersede it: the
with-instance arm is actually the **lower-variance** one on test QWK
(sd 0.0088 vs 0.0223), so that claim was wrong.*

Practical consequence: with `instance_loss_weight = 0.30` fixed and 3 seeds, the ablation
cannot separate the arms. A tuning sweep over `instance_loss_weight ∈ {0.1, 0.3, 0.5}`
with validation-based selection — and more seeds — is the only way to give CLAM its best
shot before concluding anything about the loss. Until it is run the conclusion is
"no measurable benefit at the default weight on this cohort".

Two consequences that must be in the limitations:

1. **No thumbnail overlays and no mask-based overlap score.** The feature-only pipeline has
   no slide thumbnails and no tissue masks, so `attention_mask_overlap` cannot be computed.
   Heatmaps are coordinate-space attention scatter, evaluated visually and by entropy, not
   by IoU against annotated tumour regions.
2. **The ablation's localisation question is answered by entropy, not by overlap.** Attention
   sharpness (mean entropy, normalised entropy, top-10 attention share) is the quantitative
   proxy used here; it answers "does the instance loss concentrate attention?" not "does it
   land on tumour?".

## 9. Limitations

* Research prototype; **not a clinical tool**. No external validation.
* Trained and tested on a PANDA subset only; performance will not transfer unchanged to other
  scanners, labs, or staining protocols without external hospital validation.
* **ISUP class imbalance** makes accuracy misleading. Accuracy ~0.39 with QWK ~0.70–0.74
  means the model gets the ordinal direction right far more often than it gets the exact
  class right; it collapses predictions towards the frequent low grades.
* Test set is 60 slides. Every confidence interval in this report is wide, and no claim of
  significance is made where an interval spans zero.
* One feature extractor, one split, three seeds. Person 1's ABMIL baseline and this CLAM-SB
  share features and split by design, so their comparison is paired — but it is still one
  feature representation.
* Attention heatmaps are **interpretability cues, not guaranteed segmentations**.
* The `instance_loss_weight` sweep and the ABMIL seeds 1–2 paired comparison are still open
  (see §9); the conclusions above hold at the single default weight and seed 0 respectively.
